## 4. Documentary Narrative & Story Arcs

The documentary explores the 30-year history of geodesic and fiberglass dome shelters as an immediate, dignified, and controversial response to America's homelessness crisis:

1. **The Origins (1985–1993)**: Activist **Ted Hayes** sets up *Justiceville* in downtown Los Angeles, goes on a 35-day hunger strike, and partners with ARCO to erect 20 fiberglass Omni-Sphere domes on Golden Avenue.
2. **The Architecture**: Craig Chamberlain's 20-foot, 21-panel fiberglass **Omni-Sphere**, rooted in Buckminster Fuller's geodesic dome concepts (and Walther Bauersfeld's 1926 planetarium).
3. **The Daily Life (1993–2006)**: Curved walls with no corners, rain drumming on thin fiberglass, communal kitchen and shared bathrooms, 12 homes sheltering up to 34 people in a self-governed community.
4. **The Closure (2006)**: After 13 years, the landlord increases rent from $2,500/mo to $18,000/mo. The village is unbolted and sold off on October 31, 2006.
5. **The Modern Evolution**: InterShelter (Juneau, Alaska), First Assembly of God's dome village in Honolulu, Dignity Village in Portland, and Alan Graham's 51-acre Community First! Village in Austin, Texas.
6. **The Reality of Aging Homelessness**: The shifting demographics (half of single homeless adults are 50+, veterans crisis, mortality rates) and the debate between immediate shelter ("tourniquet") vs permanent housing ("cure").
